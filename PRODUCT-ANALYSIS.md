## 1. What the company actually is

Ferrocare is a **Pune-based OEM of oil purification and fluid filtration machinery**, not a trading house. Everything in the range is built around one industrial problem: hydraulic, gear, turbine and lube oil degrades long before it needs to be replaced, because of solid particles, water, sludge and varnish.

The company was incorporated in 1980 and its founding technology — **electrostatic liquid cleaning** — is still the centre of the range and the reason it is best known. Over four decades the portfolio widened to cover the rest of the contamination problem: vacuum dehydration for water, mechanical filtration for bulk solids, coolant systems for grinding, and instruments for measuring whether any of it worked.

**Two sites are referenced in public listings:**
- `ferrocare.net` — a full product catalogue hosted on IndiaMART (structured product pages with published specifications). This is the substantive technical source.
- `ferrocare.com` — a Wix-hosted marketing site (thin content; mostly a landing page).

**Strategic read:** the company sells into heavy industry with a technically-literate, engineer-to-engineer motion. It is a specification-led business — most units are built to order against a duty point rather than sold from stock.

## 2. Product portfolio — eleven families, 62 documented models

### 2.1 Electrostatic Liquid Cleaners (ELC series) — *the core product*
**Technology:** Two stainless-steel electrode sets are charged positive and negative by a special transformer. Suspended particles — metallic and non-metallic alike — migrate to the electrode of opposite polarity and are collected on pleated cellulose media, where the deformed field is strongest. No chemical reaction, so the additive package is untouched.

**Why it matters:** reaches **1 micron and below** (theoretically to 0.01 µ), and removes **sludge and varnish**, which mechanical filters do not register as particles at all. Servo valves, pumps and sliding mechanisms are the direct beneficiaries.

**Models:** ELC 25 A · ELC 50 C · ELC 50 C Balancing Tank · ELC 50 C Dehydration Cell · ELC 100 LP · ELC 100 LP (10 LPM) · Electrostatic Coalescer 10 LPM · Electrostatic Lube Cleaner

**Representative specification (ELC 100 LP):** 1,500 LPH flow · 12 LPM pump · 1,000 L oil capacity (extendable 3,000 L) · 600 W · 380–415 V 3-phase · 181 kg · 900 × 615 × 920 mm

**Operating envelope:** mineral base oils only (not IC engine oil) · max 500 ppm moisture · max 60 °C · synthetic specification available on request

**Assessment:** This is a genuine differentiator. Electrostatic cleaning competes where mechanical filtration cannot reach, and the "no additive damage" property is the commercial argument that lets customers reclaim oil instead of buying new. The ELC 50 C is the volume model; the 100 LP serves central systems and turbine lube oil.

---

### 2.2 Low Vacuum Dehydration Machines (LVDH series)
**Technology:** Oil is heated under controlled low vacuum. At reduced pressure water's boiling point collapses, so moisture flashes off at temperatures the oil tolerates. A refrigerated condenser recovers the vapour; a multi-stage filter train removes residual solids.

**Capability:** removes **100 % of free water plus emulsified moisture**, to a final water content of **10–20 ppm**. Handles viscosity up to **320 cSt** and water content as high as **20,000 ppm** — i.e. oil that is more water than lubricant. Vacuum to **1–5 mbar**.

**Models:** LVDH 600 · LVDH 900 · LVDH 2100 · Vacuum Dehydration Oil Purifier · Vacuum Dehydration Unit · Lubrication LVDH (320 cSt) · Electrostatic Oil Dehydration System

**Capacity range:** 600 LPH → 6,000 LPH. Heater loads 6 kW → 24 kW. Skid or trolley mounted; MS or SS construction.

**Notable:** the **LVDH 900 adds degassification** — specified for turbine lube and EH control oil where entrained gas matters as much as water. The **Electrostatic Oil Dehydration System** is the flagship combination: electrostatic cleaning and vacuum dehydration in one machine (1,000 LPH, 10 ppm final water, 5 mbar).

**Assessment:** This is where the largest customer savings sit. A turbine lube reservoir holds thousands of litres; reclaiming it instead of replacing it is a directly quantifiable win. The 6,000 LPH ceiling means Ferrocare can serve central systems, not just individual machines.

### 2.3 Hydraulic & Lube Oil Filtration Machines
**Technology:** Magnetic pre-straining pins ferrous wear debris, then progressively finer cartridge elements in a multi-stage train. Staging multiplies element life and holds differential pressure low. Units couple to the reservoir and filter while the machine runs.

**Models:** Hydraulic Oil Filtration Machine (Magnetic Strainer) · Automatic Oil Filtration Machine · Filtration Unit with Multistage Filtration for FRF Oil · Portable Hydraulic Oil Filtration Machine · Hydraulic Oil Filtration Trolley · Lube Oil Treatment Machine · Injection Moulding Oil Filtration System

**Capability:** single-pass filtration to **3–5 µm**; trolley and portable formats let one unit service many machines.

**Assessment:** The commodity end of the range, but strategically necessary — it is the entry product that gets Ferrocare onto a plant floor, and the FRF variant is a specialised, higher-margin niche tied to steel and power.

### 2.5 Gear Box Oil Filtration Systems
**Technology:** Couples directly to the gearbox drain and fill points, drawing from the low point of the sump where deposits collect. Continuous circulation lifts carbonaceous sludge into suspension and captures it — **while the gearbox runs under load**.

**Specification:** 3,000 LPH · automatic · 3-phase · specified for steel, power, cement, heavy engineering, automotive, ports and shipping.

**Assessment:** A tightly-defined, high-value niche. "No dismantling required" and "removes carbon deposits more effectively than drain-and-refill" are strong, specific claims against a real maintenance cost.

---

### 2.6 Coolant Filtration Systems
**Specification:** **400 LPM** flow · 500 LPH capacity · mild steel · 240/415 V · automatic.

**Applications:** surface, centreless, internal/external and gear grinding, honing, superfinishing.

**Assessment:** A deliberate adjacency — same customer (machine shops), different fluid. The engineering argument is sound: recirculated abrasive fines scratch the workpiece just as surely as the wheel does, so filtration *is* surface-finish control.

### 2.8 Oil Testing Kits
- **Contaminated Oil Patch Test Kit** — membrane test read against a chart. Parameters: water content, particle count, viscosity, moisture, dielectric, TAN. Portable set with pipette, syringe and glassware. 18-month shelf life.
- **Oil Contamination Testing Kit** — syringe sampling, chemical test strips for TAN, viscosity, acidity, water content. Covers transformer, engine and hydraulic oils.
- **Contamination Oil Testing Kit (Combination)** — bench kit, 220 V / 50 Hz.

**Assessment:** Low-ticket, high-frequency consumables that create recurring customer contact and feed the sampling habit that justifies larger equipment.

### 2.9 Two-Stage & Three-Stage Mechanical Filtration
**Technology:** A magnetic pre-strainer pins the ferrous fraction with no consumable at all, then progressively finer cartridge elements take the bulk, the fine silt and finally polish. Staging means no single element blinds off early, so differential pressure stays low and the outlet cleanliness code stays consistent rather than drifting as an element loads.

**Specification:** 75 LPM at 10 bar on the two-stage unit; elements from 1, 3, 5, 10, 25 and 40 µm. The water-glycol machine (75 LPM, 16 bar, two-stage) is built for HFC fluid that standard filtration cannot handle, and the FRF multistage unit covers fire-resistant circuits.

### 2.10 Consumables & Tool Steels
- **Dust Collector Paper for ELC Machines** — cellulose media, 126 m² filtering area, 3,000 m³/hr airflow, 50+ filters. **This is the razorblade**: every installed ELC machine consumes collector media indefinitely.
- **Nachi High Speed Steels** — grades M2 / M35 / M42 & ASP, powder-metallurgy route, 5 % Co, 30–35 HRC, from 0.3 mm dia, 2,000 mm length.
- **High Speed Steel Rod** — M42, 8 mm dia, 62–64 HRC, ground, round/flat, under 3 m.

**Assessment:** The steel trading line is **unrelated to the core filtration business** and looks like legacy or opportunistic trade. It dilutes positioning and, if the website is anything to go by, confuses the value proposition. Worth separating hard in any rebrand.

## 5. Competitive assessment

**Strengths**
1. **Genuine proprietary technology** — electrostatic collection to sub-micron including sludge and varnish is not a commodity capability.
2. **Breadth across the whole contamination problem** — particles, water, gas, sludge, in one supplier.
3. **Closed measurement loop** — Ferrocare sells the machines *and* the instruments that prove they work. Most competitors sell one or the other.
4. **Engineer-to-order** — custom sizing is the norm, not an exception. Defensible against low-cost catalogue sellers.
5. **45-year trading history** in Indian heavy industry, with export capability (IEC held).
6. **Consumables annuity** — collector media and elements generate recurring revenue from the installed base.

**Weaknesses / risks**
1. **Digital presence is weak and fragmented** — the substantive listings live on a third-party IndiaMART microsite; the company's own `.com` is a thin Wix landing page; and a **third domain (ferrocaremachine.com) exists for the same GST and address**. Three properties split search authority three ways.
2. **Product naming is inconsistent** — the same machine is listed under several near-duplicate names ("Electrostatic Liquid Cleaning Machine" vs "Electrostatic Liquid Cleaner"; two separate "High Speed Steel Rod" entries). This reads as an unmanaged catalogue.
3. **Published specifications conflict** — the same ELC 100 LP listing shows 250, 500 and 1,500 LPH, and 250 W vs 600 W. The LVDH-600 is 390 kg in the catalogue and 250 kg on IndiaMART. Procurement engineers notice this.
4. **The high-speed steel line is off-strategy** and dilutes a clean filtration positioning.
5. **Services are invisible** — on-site cleaning, oil analysis and AMC are the highest-value activities and are barely represented.
6. **The best products are the least visible** — the integrated I-series, the IoT/MODBUS R-series, the EPT phosphate-ester systems, Magnetix separators and the S120 imaging counter all exist and are all effectively absent from the indexed web.
7. **The best specification data is unpublished** — the ELC selection guide is the single most persuasive document the company owns, and it exists only inside a PDF.
8. **Small organisation (26–50 people)** against larger filtration OEMs; capacity to serve large multi-site accounts is a question mark.
9. **No current certification is published** — ISO 9001 is claimed in literature, but no in-date certificate is verifiable, which matters in power and steel procurement.
10. **The client roster is a hidden asset** — a documented supply history including Tata Motors, Tata Steel, Maruti, Bajaj Auto, BHEL, NTPC, SAIL, HAL and DRDO appears nowhere on the current site.

**Opportunities**
- Package **"clean, measure, maintain"** as a subscription-style oil management programme rather than selling discrete machines.
- Lead with **on-site cleaning as a paid audit** that converts to equipment sales.
- Target **power generation and steel** with the degassification LVDH and EH oil units — highest value per unit, most quantifiable ROI.
- Publish **ISO cleanliness code case studies** ("before 21/19/16, after 16/14/11") — this is the language the buying engineer uses.

---

## 6. Summary

Ferrocare is a **technically credible, engineer-led manufacturer with a real proprietary differentiator** (electrostatic cleaning), a defensible breadth of range, and a genuinely valuable consumables annuity. It is held back not by its engineering but by **fragmented digital presence, inconsistent product naming, conflicting published data, and an almost invisible services business**.

The single highest-leverage move available to the company is to consolidate its product identity and put its measurement-and-service capability in front of customers as the reason to buy — which is what this website has been built to do.

**Process detail worth marketing:** under vacuum, water boils **below 53 °C**. ### 7.3 Products missing from the public listings

Three significant product lines exist in the catalogues but are effectively invisible on the indexed web:

1. **Integrated ELC + LVDH units (I-600 / I-900 / I-2100)** — electrostatic cleaning and vacuum dehydration on one frame, with automatic moisture ppm and NAS/ISO readout on a self-diagnostic touchscreen. This is the answer to the most common real-world case (oil that is both dirty *and* wet) and it is not findable.
2. **The ELC R-series with IoT and MODBUS** — live condition data to PC or Android. A genuinely modern capability that the website does not mention at all.
3. **EPT Canada phosphate-ester / EHC systems** — acid number held **below 0.2 mg KOH/g**, moisture below 300–500 ppm, resistivity restored, oxygen and combustible gases removed, built in India under EPT Canada chemistry.
4. **Magnetix magnetic separators** — rare-earth magnets giving **up to 8× ferrite force**, dry cake discharge (no consumables), 20–500 LPM.
5. **S120 digital imaging particle counter** — images each particle from **4 µm to >100 µm**, classifies fatigue / sliding / cutting wear, fibres, water and air bubbles, and **eliminates bubbles from the count**. A serious diagnostic instrument, entirely absent from the marketing.

### 7.5 Conflicts and gaps — not published as fact

1. **Current ISO certificate** — version, body, scope and expiry unverified.
2. **Audited financials** — the ₹25–100 Cr band is self-declared on IndiaMART; public MIRA INFORM credit reports are deliberately obfuscated and were not extractable.
